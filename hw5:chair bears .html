on the long expedition from coast to coast, comander Meriweather Lewis and William Clark experianced many dangers.

one night, commander lewis heard scuffling and chewing. they had had plenty of bear attacks, so he knew a bear eating when he heard one. from the sound it was impossible to tell what it was eating, but from the sounds it was almost surely brown.
he creeped up on it, and saw a huge, black bear chewing woodily on a chair. the mangled remains of others showed that yet more furniture had secumbed to the jaws of the monster.

upon closer inspection, commander lewis realized the creature was not attacking the camp out of malice, but rather out of an insatiable hunger for joinery. the beast paid no mind to the salted meats or the visual threats of the men. instead, it focused entirely on devouring a mahogany rocking chair with the enthusiasm of a cub in a blackberry patch.
william clark joined him soon after, musket drawn, but stopped in sheer bewilderment as the beast happily licked the varnish off a nearby footstool. they realized then that these "chair bears" were a entirely new species of gentle, woodworking-obsessed giants.
by morning, the camp's seating arrangements were completely decimated, replaced only by a pile of sawdust and three incredibly bloated, purring bears. lewis noted in his private diary that the creatures seemed to prefer oak over pine, a detail he felt thomas jefferson's sophisticated palate might appreciate, though the public would surely deem it madness.

the men soon learned that the bears were not dangerous to humans unless a human happened to be wearing wooden clogs or sitting on a particularly appetizing cedar bench. clark spent the next three days conducting experiments, offering the bears various camp items to map their dietary preferences.
he found that while standard wild bears would fight over fish or berries, a chair bear would completely ignore a fresh salmon if a well-crafted maple writing desk was nearby. the bears possessed specialized, chisel-like teeth that could shave through hard timber with terrifying efficiency, yet they would use their heavy paws to delicately balance a table leg before snapping it in two like a twig.
as the expedition pushed further west, the chair bears secretly followed the trail of the men, acting as a strange sort of cleanup crew. any broken wagon wheel or discarded crate left along the path vanished before the next sunrise, digested by the ever-hungry beasts. the men even began to form attachments to them, calling the largest one 'old chippendale' and using the soft, purring creatures as makeshift pillows during cold nights on the hard ground.
